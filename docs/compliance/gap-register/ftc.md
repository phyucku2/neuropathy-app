# FTC Gap Register (Health Breach Notification Rule, FTC Act §5, COPPA)

> **This is a gap register prepared by engineering — NOT legal advice, an FTC filing, or a claim of compliance.** Regime classification (HIPAA vs FTC HBNR), COPPA applicability, and claim-substantiation determinations rest with qualified counsel. See [README](./README.md) for the honest product status and legend.

## Summary

This is a remediation gap register, not a compliance attestation — the product processes **synthetic data only**, has no production deployment, no signed BAAs, and is not FDA-cleared. The dominant FTC exposure is structural: the entire compliance pack is HIPAA-framed around a covered entity, yet ADR-0003 keeps a live B2C self-registration path (`POST /auth/register`, verified in code) that would make the app a PHR vendor subject to the **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule** — a regime with no pathway anywhere in the repo. On the deception axis, the code is largely honest and several claims are genuinely met-in-code (no analytics/ad SDKs verified in the dependency tree, non-diagnostic disclaimer present and tested, patient-held sharing with symmetric revoke/delete), but two claims outrun reality: the "refuses unencrypted connections" security promise ships in the UI while TLS is unconfigured (staging has no TLS), and registration collects health-account data with no privacy notice or affirmative consent and no age screen, leaving COPPA applicability undetermined.

**Correction applied (per adversarial review):** the `share_with_clinic` capability is **opt-out, not opt-in** — `backend/app/services/capability.py` defines its spec with `default=True` and returns the default (True) when no patient row exists. The earlier "OFF by default" framing was factually wrong; a default-ON sensitive-health-data disclosure is itself an FTC consent-clarity/unfairness consideration, reflected in the rows below.

## What this lens does NOT assert

- It does **not** assert the product is FTC-compliant, nor make the legal regime determination (HIPAA covered-entity vs FTC HBNR PHR vendor) — that rests with counsel and is the P0 open question.
- It gives no legal advice as a conclusion; COPPA applicability, HBNR applicability, and claim substantiation are questions routed to qualified counsel.
- "met-in-code" describes what the code and dependency tree demonstrably do today (e.g., no analytics SDKs, tested disclaimers); it does not guarantee future states or the accuracy of external marketing/store copy not in this repo.
