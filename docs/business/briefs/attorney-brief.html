Neuropathy Care Platform — Legal Brief

The legal surface: entity/IP structure, the regulatory posture the company has deliberately adopted, data-protection and consent architecture, contracts outstanding, and the open questions that need counsel's judgment. Written to brief the attorney, not to substitute for their advice.
As of: July 20, 2026 IP owner: Advanced Health & Wellness Group · Licensee/device partner: BioMech Health Data: synthetic only · no PHI processed · no BAA executed
Framing. Engineering has run the company conservatively on purpose: non-diagnostic positioning, no clinical claims, synthetic data only, and no reimbursement features built pending professional sign-off. The product is not FDA-cleared. The questions below are flagged for counsel precisely because the team has declined to answer them itself — most notably the FDA device-status determination, which is framed and deferred by design, not assumed.

1 · Entity, IP, and the partner relationship

  • IP ownership: Advanced Health & Wellness Group (AHWG) owns all intellectual property. BioMech Health is a licensee and the device/data partner (Frank Fornari, PhD). Counsel should confirm the license terms cleanly cover the software, the composite index, and any derived dataset, and that field-of-use / data-rights boundaries are explicit.
  • Recorded owner decision (2026-07-14): "BioMech is billed separately for now." BioMech's device/assessment billing is outside this initiative's scope; the software's device-status question therefore attaches primarily to this software, with the hardware relevant as a data-provenance dependency. A revisit trigger is recorded if the relationship changes.
  • Open items for counsel: confirm the operating entity and its relationship to AHWG; IP assignment from all contributors; trademark posture on the product name; and whether the dataset (the core moat) is owned by AHWG free of partner claims.

3 · Data protection, privacy & consent

  • No PHI has been processed. All data to date is synthetic. Azure staging is live but the Microsoft BAA/DPA is unsigned — a recorded gate before any real PHI. Counsel should execute/confirm the BAA and review the DPA before a pilot.
  • HIPAA-shaped architecture, pending formal validation: PHI-free audit logging on reads and writes; server-enforced, revocable, per-stream patient consent; account and data deletion with re-authentication; EMR OAuth tokens in an encrypted vault deleted on revoke and on account deletion. A BAA inventory and HIPAA ops checklist exist as living documents.
  • Consent architecture: patient-held share-with-clinic consent that clinicians cannot override; a distinct, revocable billing-evidence consent scope is specced (separate from care-sharing) but not built — its design (decision D3) awaits counsel/compliance input.
  • EMR access is patient-authorized via SMART-on-FHIR OAuth (PKCE public client, no secrets); the app is registered with Epic's sandbox. Counsel should note the patient — not the company — authorizes each EMR pull.
  • For counsel: confirm state privacy-law exposure, breach-notification obligations, the privacy policy / terms of service, and whether a covered-entity vs business-associate characterization is correct for each operating model.

5 · Liability & risk posture

  • Non-diagnostic by design — the product shows a wellness trend and repeatedly disclaims being a diagnosis; it does not triage emergencies. The specced patient↔clinician communication (ADR-0046) carries a persistent "if this is an emergency, call 911" banner and makes no emergency-triage promise — a deliberate liability-limiting choice counsel should review.
  • Enabler, not biller — the company does not submit claims; a servicing clinic does. This keeps the company out of direct False Claims Act exposure, but counsel should confirm the enablement materials never cross into advising a clinic how to bill.
  • No clinical claims, no medical advice — consistent disclaimers; counsel to confirm they are sufficient and correctly placed for the marketing surfaces.

6 · Counsel's near-term priorities

  • Route the FDA device-status determination (with a regulatory consultant) — the single highest-leverage legal action; a written opinion is already drafted for that consultant.
  • Execute/confirm the Microsoft BAA and review the DPA before any real PHI touches Azure.
  • Confirm IP and dataset ownership are clean between AHWG and BioMech, including the longitudinal dataset that is the commercial moat.
  • Review consent, privacy policy, and ToS ahead of the first pilot; shape the pilot agreement (liability, data ownership, enabler terms).
