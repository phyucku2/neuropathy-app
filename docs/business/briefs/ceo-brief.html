Neuropathy Care Platform — CEO Brief

What the company is, what is built, where the moat is, and the three gates that unlock everything downstream. Written for the chief executive setting strategy and prioritizing capital and attention.
As of: July 20, 2026 Stage: pre-seed / pre-pilot · synthetic data only Live: Azure staging (register/login verified) · Epic sandbox registered
Read this first. The product is not FDA-cleared, not clinically validated, and runs on synthetic data only; no real patient data has been processed and no Business Associate Agreement has been executed. The Neuropathy Status Index is a non-diagnostic v1, pending validation. Every reimbursement figure in this brief is a vendor-published estimate for a qualified coder to validate, not a payable rate. These are not weaknesses to hide — they are the honest starting line, and the plan below is built around closing them.

1 · The company in one paragraph

We are building a non-diagnostic, patient-owned measurement platform for diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) — the Neuropathy Status Index (NSI), a single 0–100 score (higher = better) that fuses four streams no incumbent combines: patient-reported symptoms (pain, numbness), objective balance & gait (from BioMech's device tier), real-world phone/watch mobility, and labs (HbA1c) pulled from the patient's EMR. The result is a daily trajectory instead of the episodic snapshot every competitor offers, built deliberately for the 60+ population and designed to be explainable, not a black box. Advanced Health & Wellness Group (AHWG) owns all IP; BioMech Health is a licensee and the device/data partner (Frank Fornari, PhD, CEO).

2 · What is built (the honest status)

  • A real, running application — FastAPI/Postgres backend, React web app, and an Android build (Capacitor). Register, log in, daily check-in, longitudinal record, the NSI and an explainable trajectory all work end-to-end.
  • Deployed to Azure staging — as of this week, register/login are verified live through the real cloud stack; synthetic data only, pending the Microsoft BAA/DPA.
  • EMR connectivity is live in sandbox — SMART-on-FHIR patient connect is built and the app is registered with Epic's sandbox; the first end-to-end lab pull is one Epic sync away.
  • Unusual engineering rigor for the stage — research-grade (ALCOA+) data model, HIPAA-shaped security, an adversarial internal audit already run and its top findings fixed, and a binding CI quality gate. This is a diligence asset (see the CTO brief).
  • Not yet built: clinical validation of the NSI, the reimbursement/time-capture layer, the outbound clinician loop, and anything that would require a device posture.

3 · Market position & the moat

The market is fragmented into four clusters that each solve one slice — DPN point devices (Podimetrics, Siren), diabetes apps, gait/balance biomarkers (Kinesis, Zibrio), and RPM/RTM plumbing (Limber, OneStep, Exer). No incumbent fuses ADA-aligned symptom capture + multimodal gait/balance + labs into one validated longitudinal measure. The nearest validated analog (a single-signal smartphone tracker) tops out at moderate concordance on n=17 — the ceiling a quality-first product can beat.

The moat is a proprietary longitudinal multimodal dataset, not an AI model. The composite formula and any adaptive-testing engine are replicable science; the one asset a competitor cannot copy, buy, or reverse-engineer is the accumulating multi-stream record of real patients over time — which is why getting to a consented pilot is the single most valuable near-term milestone. The AI multimodal-fusion advantage is a lead to prove, not a proven finding.

5 · The revenue thesis (real, but gated)

The reimbursement path is Remote Therapeutic Monitoring (RTM) — our musculoskeletal gait/balance stream plus patient-reported function fit the RTM device-supply and treatment-management codes, and RTM uniquely permits patient self-report. Vendor estimates for comparable products run roughly $100+/patient/month (assertion pending coder validation — not a payable rate). But this is double-gated: it needs the FDA determination (RTM data must come from a device) and a certified-coder + counsel sign-off, and the time-capture tooling it requires is not built. We are an enabler, not a biller: a servicing clinic bills; we capture and export evidence.

6 · Funding path

The strategy is non-dilutive first: NIH SBIR/STTR mapped to NINDS/NIDDK/NIA/NIBIB (Phase I ≈ $150–300k feasibility; Phase II ≈ $1–2M), to fund exactly the validation the product needs. In parallel: BioMech as the natural first strategic conversation, clinic pilot Letters of Intent (highest-leverage de-risking), and modest angel/SAFE bridge capital. Seed VC comes after a deployed pilot + the FDA determination + early reimbursement evidence — engineering is ahead; regulatory clarity and traction are the gaps.

7 · Your three highest-leverage decisions

  • Engage the FDA regulatory consultant (D2). One written opinion unblocks the most — the brief is already written for them (fda-regulatory-consultant-brief.md).
  • Confirm the Azure DPA/BAA with counsel so a consented pilot can begin — the pilot is what starts the dataset moat and satisfies grant/VC "real data" milestones.
  • Green-light the SBIR track (DUNS/UEI + the validation study design) — it funds the #1 gap (measurement rigor) without dilution.

Everything else (the reimbursement build, the clinician loop, iOS) is correctly sequenced behind these three.
