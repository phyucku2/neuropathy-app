Neuropathy Care Platform — CFO Brief

The money view: what has been spent, what it costs to run, where revenue could come from and what gates it, the funding path, and the numbers a finance owner should not take at face value yet. Written for whoever owns the model, the runway, and the burn.
As of: July 20, 2026 Stage: pre-seed / pre-revenue · synthetic data only Cash cost to date: effectively cloud + tooling only (see §1)
Read this first — no revenue exists, and every dollar figure here is an estimate, not a booking. The product is pre-revenue, not FDA-cleared, not clinically validated, and runs on synthetic data only. The reimbursement numbers are vendor-published estimates for a qualified coder and counsel to validate, not payable rates, and they are double-gated (an FDA device determination and a certified-coder sign-off) before a single claim is possible. Do not put any revenue line in a board model without those sign-offs. What is real today is the cost side and the funding path.

1 · What has been spent (and what it will cost to run)

The build to date is overwhelmingly engineering time, not cash outlay. There is no deployed production, no paid clinical study, no FDA submission, and no revenue infrastructure — each of those is a future, gated spend, not a sunk one. Direct cash costs so far are limited to cloud, tooling, and store/registration fees.

2 · The revenue thesis — real shape, but gated and unbuilt

The intended revenue engine is Remote Therapeutic Monitoring (RTM): the platform's gait/balance stream plus patient-reported function map onto RTM device-supply and treatment-management codes, and RTM is notable for permitting patient self-report. Vendor estimates for comparable products run on the order of $100+/patient/month (assertion pending coder validation — not a payable rate). Three hard constraints sit in front of any of that becoming money:

  • We are an enabler, not a biller. A servicing clinic (a covered entity) bills; the platform captures and exports the evidence. Modeled revenue is therefore a SaaS/enablement fee or per-patient-per-month arrangement with clinics, not a claims stream we control.
  • Double gate. RTM data must come from an FDA "device" (§201(h)) and the coding must be blessed by a certified coder + counsel. Neither is resolved.
  • The capture tooling isn't built. The interactive-time ledger, adherence counter, and evidence export are specced and deliberately not started — they are gated on the compliance sign-off packet (D1/D3) and the FDA determination (D2).

5 · The financial moat

The defensible asset is a proprietary longitudinal multimodal dataset — the accumulating per-patient record of symptoms + gait/balance + labs over time — not the scoring formula (which is replicable science). That reframes the near-term financial objective: the highest-return use of the next dollar is getting to a consented pilot, because that is what starts the one asset a competitor cannot buy, and it is the milestone both grants and a future priced round underwrite.

6 · The CFO's watch-list

  • Keep revenue out of the model until D1+D2 clear. Any per-patient RTM figure is currently a citation, not a forecast. Model it as an option, not a line.
  • Protect runway; the burn is small — keep it that way. The big spends (validation, FDA, iOS) should each be triggered by a funding event, not run ahead of one.
  • Back up the cloud secrets and track cloud spend. Export the Azure secrets to a password manager; watch staging cost so it doesn't drift before a pilot justifies it.
  • Entity + grant readiness is cheap and on the critical path. SAM.gov/UEI registration and study design gate the first non-dilutive dollars — start them now.
