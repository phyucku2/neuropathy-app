Orientation for the Reimbursement Compliance Review

This brief orients the reviewing compliance professional before opening the formal sign-off packet (docs/product/reimbursement-signoff-packet.md). It summarizes the product, the decisions requested, the evidence that exists, and the open risks — so the credentialed review is fast and focused. It is prepared by the engineering side and is not billing, coding, or legal advice; every determination below is yours to make, not ours to assert.
Requesting owner: Advanced Health and Wellness Group (IP holder) Prepared: July 20, 2026 Review object: the sign-off packet + the reimbursed-apps comparison

1 · What the product is, in compliance terms

  • A non-diagnostic tracking and decision-support platform for neuropathy patients: patient-reported daily function (ADL check-ins), lab results imported from the patient's EMR with the patient's OAuth consent (SMART on FHIR), and balance/gait metrics parsed from BioMech assessment reports — fused into an explainable trend (direction, confidence, named signals).
  • Posture: enabler, not biller. The intended model is that a servicing clinic — a covered entity — would bill; the platform would capture and evidence. The platform holder does not submit claims.
  • Recorded owner decision (2026-07-14): "BioMech is billed separately for now." BioMech's own device/assessment billing is outside this initiative's scope. Consequence: the live device-status question attaches primarily to this software (SaMD analysis), with the BioMech hardware relevant only as a data-provenance dependency. Revisit trigger recorded if the relationship changes.
  • Status honestly stated: synthetic data only to date; not FDA-cleared or -listed; HIPAA-aligned architecture pending formal validation. The app is now deployed to Azure staging (register/login verified) and registered with Epic's sandbox, but no BAA/DPA has been executed, so no real PHI has been processed. No reimbursement-specific features have been built — that build is deliberately blocked on your sign-off.

3 · Regulatory landscape snapshot (all citations in the packet)

  • The device requirement: CMS requires RTM/RPM data to come from a "medical device as defined by the FDA" (§201(h); CY2022 PFS final rule, 86 FR 65115). Whether/how that determination is adjudicated is explicitly labeled an inference for your confirmation in the packet — we found no CMS statement assigning it, which is precisely why D2 routes to a regulatory professional.
  • 2026 code changes (new lower-threshold RTM/RPM codes) are summarized with primary-source citations; the proposed Wave 4 capture is parameterized so day-count/minute thresholds are configuration, not code.
  • Market evidence (9 products reviewed, vendor/FDA-database verified): every product with vendor-documented RTM billing and an objective data stream holds at least an FDA listing for its own software; the closest balance-measurement comparable (Sway Medical, 510(k)-cleared twice) monetizes through point-in-time testing codes rather than RTM; the pure software-led RTM cohort (Limber, Exer, OneStep) is our structural comparable set — per the owner's BioMech decision.

5 · Open risks needing your judgment

  • FDA/SaMD status (D2) — the gating unknown. The packet lays out the factual inputs a regulatory consultant needs; the comparison shows the market's answer-shape.
  • Incident-to / general-supervision questions for any clinic operating model, and the 2026 code-family selection — assertion set A1–A8 is where the CPC's red pen belongs.
  • Staging deployed, but no BAA/DPA executed yet — an Azure staging environment now exists and Epic sandbox is registered, but the Microsoft BAA/DPA is unsigned and nothing processes real PHI today, so your review remains prospective, not remedial. Executing the BAA before first real PHI is a recorded gate.
  • What we do NOT ask: no one is asked to bless current billing (there is none), and nothing in the product coaches a clinic to bill — the docs are explicit that enablement ≠ authorization.
